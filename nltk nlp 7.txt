import nltk
from nltk.tokenize import sent_tokenize,word_tokenize
from nltk.corpus import stopwords
from nltk.stem import PorterStemmer,WordNetLemmatizer
from sklearn.feature_extraction.text import CountVectorizer,TfidfVectorizer

nltk.download('punkt')
nltk.download('punkt_tab')
nltk.download('stopwords')

nltk.download('averaged_perceptron_tagger')

txt = """Tokenization is the first step in text analytics. 
The process of breaking down a text paragraph into smaller 
chunks such as words or sentences is called Tokenization."""

sentence=sent_tokenize(txt)

sentence

words=word_tokenize(txt)

words

stop_words=set(stopwords.words('english'))

stop_words

filter=[]
for i in words:
    if i not in stop_words:
        filter.append(i)

filter

ls=PorterStemmer()
stemmed_words=[]
for i in filter:
    stemmed_words.append(ls.stem(i))

stemmed_words

ls=WordNetLemmatizer()
lemm_words=[]
for i in filter:
    lemm_words.append(ls.lemmatize(i))

lemm_words

pos_tag=nltk.pos_tag(filter)

pos_tag

cv=CountVectorizer()

x=cv.fit_transform(filter)

x

x.toarray()

cv.get_feature_names_out()

x1=TfidfVectorizer()

Title
Text Preprocessing using NLTK

Objective
To perform text preprocessing operations like tokenization, stopword removal, stemming, lemmatization, POS tagging, Count Vectorization, and TF-IDF Vectorization.

Theory
Text preprocessing is an important step in Natural Language Processing. It is used to clean and convert text data into a useful format for machine learning models.
In this program, the NLTK library is used for text preprocessing. First, required NLTK packages are downloaded, such as punkt, stopwords, wordnet, and POS tagger.
A text paragraph is stored in the variable txt. Then tokenization is performed.
Sentence tokenization breaks the paragraph into separate sentences using:
sent_tokenize()
Word tokenization breaks the paragraph into separate words using:
word_tokenize()
After tokenization, stopwords are removed. Stopwords are common words like “is”, “the”, “in”, “of”, etc. These words are usually removed because they do not add much meaning.
Then stemming is performed using PorterStemmer. Stemming reduces words to their root form. For example, breaking becomes break.
After stemming, lemmatization is performed using WordNetLemmatizer. Lemmatization also converts words to base form, but it gives more meaningful words than stemming.
Next, POS tagging is applied using:
nltk.pos_tag()
POS tagging identifies the grammatical role of each word, such as noun, verb, adjective, etc.
Then Count Vectorization is used. It converts words into numerical count values.
Finally, TF-IDF Vectorization is applied. TF-IDF gives importance to words based on how frequently they appear in the text.

Short Algorithm
Step 1:
Import required libraries.
Step 2:
Download required NLTK packages.
Step 3:
Store text paragraph in a variable.
Step 4:
Perform sentence tokenization.
Step 5:
Perform word tokenization.
Step 6:
Remove stopwords from words.
Step 7:
Apply stemming on filtered words.
Step 8:
Apply lemmatization on filtered words.
Step 9:
Apply POS tagging.
Step 10:
Apply Count Vectorizer.
Step 11:
Display count vector array and feature names.
Step 12:
Apply TF-IDF Vectorizer.
Step 13:
Display TF-IDF array and feature names.
